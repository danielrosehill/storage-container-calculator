Volume price is this tool's unit for comparing what a container is worth: purchase price ÷ usable capacity, quoted in currency per usable litre, with the same figure per cubic metre alongside it. Lower is better. There is no industry-standard name for it — freight and self-storage quote per m³, supermarkets call it unit pricing, and container manufacturers quote per box — so the definition here is the tool's own. Litres are the primary scale because every container on the market lands between about 0.10 and 10 per litre, which reads more easily than four-digit figures per m³.

Volume terms. External volume is L × W × H — the space a full box occupies, and what pallet and container figures are based on. Usable capacity is what fits inside, from published internal dimensions; it is the basis for volume price. Comparing on external volume would flatter a tapered ALC, which is exactly the error this calculator exists to avoid.

Assumptions. Single-SKU pallets (no mixing box types on one pallet); no overhang; EUR‑1 pallet height 144 mm; 20 ft container internal volume 33.2 m³ holding 11 EUR‑1 pallets on the floor. Pallet counts are conservative estimates for planning, not a packing optimiser.

ALCs. Attached-lid containers include their lid, have sloping walls, and nest about 75 % into each other when empty — so they lose usable volume against a straight-walled Eurobox of the same external size but win badly on the return leg and on empty storage. Euroboxes are assumed not to nest.

Catalogue capacities are typical published figures and vary by manufacturer — check the supplier's own internal dimensions before committing to a large order.
